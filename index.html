How it works

Four promises a plain list of payments can't keep

Aino is 22 and studies in Mikkeli. She has seven subscriptions billed three different ways. Here is her journey — every screen is from the working prototype.

1

Warns before the money leaves

A push 3 days before every renewal, 7 days before a free trial converts and 14 days before a yearly charge.

2

Covers what card data can't see

App store, PayPal and family plans hide inside lump sums. Customers name them in a few taps, starting from the payment the bank can see.

3

Points to the exit

The deadline, where it's managed, how hard it is — “about 2 minutes, 4 steps” — and the official cancellation page. The customer decides; Mohar never cancels for them.

4

Confirms it stopped

Next month Mohar checks that the charge really disappeared. If one arrives anyway, it is flagged and routed to your dispute flow.

Features

Every feature answers something the evidence shows

Built for 18–28-year-olds who open their bank app a few times a week to check the balance.

Free-trial countdown

A live countdown to the moment a trial becomes a paid charge — for detected trials and ones customers add themselves.

3.6M unwanted UK subscriptions began as trials.4

“Still using it?” check-up

Once a month, one card per subscription: Keep, Stop, or Ask me next month. Postponing becomes a deliberate choice.

Over a quarter of Finns put off cancelling in case they need it later.1

Price-change alert

“Streamview went up from €10.99 to €12.99” — spotted from the bank's own transactions, with a twelve-month price history.

Only about a third of Finns check subscription spending monthly.1

Year in review

A shareable card: what the year cost, the biggest and least-used subscriptions, and what stopping saved.

US consumers underestimate their subscription spend by $133 a month on average.5

Yearly cost, side by side

“€23/month = €276/year” on every subscription, so small charges feel as large as they really are.

Costly in total, trivial one by one.

Duplicate spotting

Flags two services of the same kind — or a personal plan next to a family plan the customer already shares.

“Do you need both?” — never “you must cancel”.

Saved-so-far counter

“You've saved €23.00 since stopping 1 subscription” — the reward after the confirmation, and a figure your bank can report.

Celebrates, never scolds.

Exit difficulty hint

“About 2 minutes · 4 steps in PayPal” or “Must cancel in your phone's app store” — before the customer even starts.

40% of EU consumers found cancelling very difficult.3

Why a bank, not an app

Only your bank can see this coming

  • Balance-clash warnings

    “€23.00 charges Friday. Your balance is €18.40.” No third-party tracker knows the balance — your bank does.

  • A reason to open your app

    Warnings arrive through your existing push channel, in your brand, at the moment they matter.

  • Fewer “what is this charge?” questions

    Every recurring charge is named, dated and explained — and charges that arrive after stopping go straight into your dispute flow.

  • Ahead of the rules

    The EU's Digital Fairness Act proposal, covering subscription renewals and free trials, is expected in Q4 2026.6 The UK plans to require renewal reminders and easy exits from spring 2027.7

White-label

Your brand, not ours

One product, licensed to many banks. Your name, your colours and your name for the feature — your customers never see ours.

Built-in limits

What Mohar never does

Clear boundaries make it easy to approve — for your compliance team and for your customers.

  • Cancel a subscription on the customer's behalf
  • Block or hold a payment
  • Give budgeting, saving or investment advice
  • Touch rent, loans, insurance, utilities or phone contracts
  • Ask for or store bank login details
